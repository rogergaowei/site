The Starship Launch — Full Version

A two-day trip with Dad to watch Starship, explore South Padre Island, and build a sand fortress against the waves.

Published by Roger.

Read the short version.

November 13, 2026, 4:00 AM.

At this early time, my dad got me up. It was time to begin the journey to watch Starship, as my dad thought that we had to go early so there would be spots to view Starship launch. (Actually, he later realized that we could go later.)

Now it was time to go on the journey that had taken a lot of persuading to happen…

The persuading process

It had been hard to persuade my mom. She was into learning and wouldn't really understand what we had to say. It took playing three times more than I usually play, plus signing a paper that said what I would do during the trip, to finally persuade her to allow us to go.

I also learned that if this had been a break, she would totally support Dad and me going. But because this was during the school term, she was hesitant: since the trip was from Friday to Saturday, I missed one day of school and two extracurricular activities (Chinese and piano).

(fast-forward back to September 13, 2026, 4:00 AM.)

Finally, we could now go on the journey.

At first, we started on breakfast, which was pretty simple: just hot whole wheat bread plus milk.

When my mom came down, she was surprised to see us because she thought we'd leave at 3:00. But my dad had realized that one hour later, at 4:00, was okay.

Then we packed up our belongings, which included some snacks from Halloween trick-or-treating, and my camera that I kept everywhere. The camera was a pretty old Canon beginner camera, but I took advantage of its scroll function and managed to take a giant amount of photos, now with 168, to make an animation. Note that not all are animations; there are also images for memories.

Now it was time to go on the very long, 5-hour trip to Starbase, as I lived in Houston.

At first, I slept for 1 hour, waking up when we went from the I-610 to I-69 junction. The next time I woke up, 1 hour after the start of the journey, we were on US-77, which my dad had told me was overlapping with 69. That was really hard to understand completely.

We continued the journey, every so often going into a town where it was time for the speed limit to slow down from 75 mph to 35 mph.

Three hours into the journey, my dad was getting tired, as we left at 4:00 a.m. So we pulled into a car park in that area just for my dad to rest and sleep, and for me just to watch my favorite YouTube channel, Project Air. ()

Next, we continued the journey, seeing many restaurants along the way, and since I was getting hungry, we stopped at one of them to eat.

Passing time

On this really long 5-hour trip, I needed to pass time. Here are some of the things that I used to pass time:

1. Windshield cleaning:

Dad decided to clean the windshield pretty early into the trip, and I used that as a tool to pass time by watching the droplets from the bottom trickle up, up, up, up the windshield. You know, I never turn down a tool to pass time.

2. Wind farms:

We also passed through some wind farms. The first wind farm had pretty strong windmills spinning pretty fast, and I called them "up and down and up and down cartwheeling thingamabobs" because they basically looked a bit like very strange wheels with three spokes.

3. Palm trees:

There were also palm trees. I classified them as healthy, with a lot of dead leaves that they could feast on if the drought came, and unhealthy, which meant they had long and spindly trunks and not that many leaves alive and dead. I saw some pretty low but healthy trees, and there were also unhealthy trees. I also counted two dead palm trees with zero leaves: no dead, no alive.

Still, I got bored only in the last 30 minutes, so I was happy(and surprised) that I even managed to stay entertained! (Usually, I get bored in the first hour, so this was a confusing thing.)

Finally, we reached the Starbase's nearby island, South Padre Island, as the roads to Starbase were closed. We had arrived pretty early at 12:00, but the launch was at 4:30.

I settled up on the rocks near the coastline, occasionally going into the trenches to watch the water and feel it, and occasionally resting on the rocks to watch the waves eventually cover the low rocks as the tide increased.

I also did one lesson of Li yong le, fourth grade, which is Chinese fourth grade math. Finally, as launch time approached, it was time for me to open my camera and have it recording video. Unfortunately, launch time seemed to be delayed, so that video was just worthless waiting. Luckily, I could still delete it. Finally, I saw smoke coming out from the rocket launch tower. It had launched. I quickly started filming on my camera.

Then I finished my first clip of the video.

Next, the heavy booster was approaching landing, but it was really off target. When it reached ground level, Dad wondered if it would literally go all the way to the launch tower directly horizontally, but that question was answered when we saw a really small version of a nuclear bomb explosion onto the ground. I also managed to film this approach of the heavy booster, but later I accidentally deleted it as I failed to protect it on my camera.

Seconds later, there was the explosion sound: two giant claps, just multiplied 100 times. Later, I also saw the fires produced by it, and that made me think that it crashed on land, but they were supposed to crash in the water.

The next day, I learned that it was due to a software issue. They didn't want to risk failing to catch the heavy booster at the launch pad because that meant risking Starship's recovery, which was the main goal of this project.

November 14, 2026, approximately 10:00 a.m.

Now I woke up. I had slept all the way from 8:00 p.m. yesterday to 10:00 a.m. today, and this was pretty much a lot of sleep for me. Dad said he, on his app, had approximately 12 hours of sleep, and I had even longer than that.

This was pretty handy as we were heading home today, so Dad didn't need to stop in the car park.

Now we needed to go to breakfast, as my dad had thought there wasn't breakfast at our hotel. I told him there was, but when we arrived, breakfast had ended.

We found a place called Cafe by the Beach in a resort. When we arrived, we found out that the wait was approximately 15 minutes. Apparently the "Cafe by the Beach" thing was actually true to its word, and there was a beach nearby. I played there a bit to pass the time.

Finally, it was time for us to order. I ordered a breakfast taco, a yogurt cup with fruit and cinnamon granola, and a mango smoothie with pineapple and oat milk, too. My dad ordered something that I forgot.

Soon our meals arrived, and we had to go to the restroom to wash our hands, but it was locked. We asked for the passcode, and then, knowing it, we went to the bathroom.

Now we could eat. Apparently my breakfast taco came with some bread, and that came with strawberry jam. So as I was waiting for the breakfast taco to cool down, I settled into that little bread plus strawberry jam appetizer.

After I went to the beach, I started creating a water wave stopper system. I had also noticed there were little holes everywhere, and I realized covering them up still would not completely block them from going up.

I decided to start with the front wall of what I called the fortress. At that time the tide had not risen, so the first waves managed to go over the wall, but after that it was basically not much. I also needed a good place to mine without getting wet sand, so I started on the side walls and the side moats, though the moats would not really do much. As that was happening, the tide was picking up, and some waves managed to go into the little courtyard. But after some time and some mining of the sand at my dad's feet (which was pretty good), I closed it up. Now I had a good courtyard.

The tide was picking up. Soon some waves came and broke through parts of the walls, but thanks to the old waves' sediment deposits, I always had a ready bunch of sediment to use. There was also the courtyard, which basically rose off the ground, so I always had an enclosed space of sand to mine from. That helped me build up the walls.

Apparently one of the waves left pretty much only the back wall standing and everything else down, but it still survived, and quickly repaired walls went back up. Finally, one more series of waves critically damaged the fortress, and then another damaged it again, and another damaged it again. Soon the fortress called it quits. Still, it had lasted pretty long: around an hour in all of the waves, which made me really proud.

Also, I had blocked off one of the holes with a giant amount of sand, but after 10 minutes it had chewed its way through the sand and burst through the surface. More waves only left the area around the hole standing. Did that hole somehow strengthen its nearby sand? When I tested it on my wall, it acted like regular sand. That gave me something to wonder about, and it was vvvvveeeeeeeerrrrrrrrryyyyy confusing.

Soon, after some business had been done (getting rid of the sand and changing into dry pants (this left me a bit cold and queasy (remember, I had a large meal before))), we went to the starship wreckage. This time was a bit farther than the old one, so I had to walk 15 minutes faster than my normal speed over to the rocks that I set camp.

image credits: Dad

After some waiting, when we couldn't see Starship, we heard a large sound above, like the sound of an explosion. It surprised me, as I didn’t know what was happening, so it was like hearing a large clap while blindfolded.

At first, I thought Starship had crashed and exploded, but there were other possibilities:

1. The ship lighting its engines (though I think that would be too much sound)

2. Breaking the sound barrier after going from supersonic speeds to subsonic speeds (that one may be more likely)

Soon we saw Starship, and this time, unlike what my dad had predicted, it wasn’t coming from the west and instead was going straight down.*

Anyways, back to the launch. Starship had come in really fast, and now had lit its engines. After some recorrection, the ship managed to get caught! The goal of the mission was to recapture the ship, and that had actually happened! I felt amazed.

Finally, it was time to go home. We walked back to our car’s parking lot, but we had forgotten where we parked our car. My dad, using the remote control, had unlocked the car, and by some strange coincidence, we were close enough to the car (about 5 feet) to see the car lights light up.

As there was a long line of cars going out (they all were from the starship launch), and all the users made wi-fi very slow so I couldn’t do this summary, I did my Chinese homework in the car, waiting for the line of cars to go away for less traffic. Finally, as I wanted to play the game 2048, my dad decided to pull out. That got us going on the journey.

On the road, I also had to pass time like how I did on the journey to the Starbase area. These are the things I did to pass the journey:

1. Journal: I wrote a lot of this journal in the car.

2. Windmills: this time, the windmills were spinning much stronger than how I came, with all of them spinning at a pretty high speed.

3. Windshield cleaning: the same thing as what we did on the journey there.

4. Snacks: I also ate snacks to pass some time too, including some snacks my mom brought back from China.

This time, I got bored easily, but I managed to fall asleep after some boredom.

This time I asked my dad if he needed to go to the car park to rest. He didn't, due to the rest and sleep he got from Friday night to Saturday morning. We also stopped by a restaurant this time for dinner.

Soon, at 8, it was time for me to sleep so we would arrive at 10. I fell asleep pretty quickly, and I woke up when there were only a few minutes remaining in our journey, once we were already in my area or neighborhood.

When I arrived, still drowsy, I walked up to my bedroom and slept from there all the way to Sunday at 7. The journey had ended, but still, I have great memories of this Starship mission.

Notes

We had actually thought about the reasons why ships always went to the east and then came in from the west.

My first theory was that satellites, like the GPS and Starlink satellites, needed to stay in the same way as the Earth spun, so they had to have the rocket going east.

Another one was that if they were going both ways, they might crash into each other, so the GPS and Starlink satellites may force the other satellites to orbit their way.

The final one is actually the speed to gain Earth orbit. Earth spins at around half a kilometer each second, and you need 7.9 kilometers per second to gain orbit. The Earth's speed allows it to actually be 7.4 kilometers each second to gain Earth orbit, but if you were launching the other way, it would be 8.4 kilometers per second. Think about that!
