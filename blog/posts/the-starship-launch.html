The Starship Launch

A two-day trip with Dad to watch Starship, explore South Padre Island, and build a sand fortress against the waves.

Published by Roger.

November 13, 2026

Dad woke me up at 4:00 a.m. It was finally time for our trip to watch Starship!

Getting permission had taken some persuading. Mom was worried because the trip meant missing a day of school, Chinese class, and piano. I practiced three times as much as usual and signed a paper promising what I would do during the trip. Eventually, she agreed to let us go.

After a simple breakfast, we packed snacks and my old Canon camera and set off from Houston. I wanted to take plenty of photos and videos to remember the trip.

The drive was long, so I slept for part of it. When I was awake, I watched water droplets travel up the windshield, spotted palm trees, and looked at wind farms. The spinning turbines looked like strange wheels with three spokes. I called them “up and down and up and down cartwheeling thingamabobs.” You know, I never turn down a way to pass the time.

Dad got tired along the way, so we stopped for him to rest while I watched Project Air, my favorite YouTube channel. Later, we stopped for food before continuing south. I only got bored during the last thirty minutes of the drive. I felt happy and surprised that I had stayed entertained for so long—usually I get bored in the first hour!

We reached South Padre Island around noon. The roads to Starbase were closed, so we watched from across the water. The launch was scheduled for 4:30 p.m., which gave us plenty of time to wait.

I settled on the rocks along the coast, explored the gaps between them, and watched the rising tide cover the lower rocks. I also completed a fourth-grade Chinese math lesson.

As launch time approached, I got my camera ready. At first, I recorded nothing but waiting because the launch seemed to be delayed. Then I saw smoke rising from the launch tower. Starship was launching! I quickly started filming.

Later, we watched the booster descend. It looked far from the tower, and Dad wondered whether it could still move sideways to reach it. Then we saw a huge burst that looked like an explosion. Seconds later, two enormous claps reached us. Imagine clapping your hands, but a hundred times louder!

I filmed the booster’s approach, too, but accidentally deleted that clip later. I wished I had protected it on my camera.

After such an early start and a long day, I went to sleep around 8:00 p.m.

November 14, 2026

I woke up around 10:00 a.m. after about fourteen hours of sleep. Dad had slept a lot, too, which was good because we would be driving home that evening.

We missed breakfast at the hotel, so we went to Cafe by the Beach. It really was beside a beach! While we waited for a table, I played in the sand. For breakfast, I ordered a taco, yogurt with fruit and cinnamon granola, and a mango smoothie with pineapple and oat milk.

Afterward, I returned to the beach and started building a fortress to stop the waves. I began with a front wall, then added side walls, moats, and a courtyard. As the tide rose, waves broke through parts of the walls, and I kept repairing them with sand from the courtyard and the deposits left by earlier waves.

One wave knocked down almost everything except the back wall, but I rebuilt it. More waves came, and I repaired the damage again and again. Eventually, I gave up, but the fortress had lasted about an hour against the waves! I felt really proud that my fortress had lasted so long.

I also noticed little holes in the sand. I covered one with a large pile of sand, but about ten minutes later, something had dug its way back to the surface. After more waves washed through, the sand around the hole was still standing. Had the hole somehow strengthened the sand nearby? When I tried using that sand in my wall, it behaved like ordinary sand. That gave me something to wonder about. It was very confusing!

After brushing off the sand and changing into dry pants, we walked to another viewing spot to watch Starship return. We waited on the rocks. Before we could see the ship, we heard a loud sound overhead, almost like an explosion. I wondered what had caused it. Changing out of my wet pants left me a bit cold and queasy after my large meal. The sudden sound surprised me, like hearing a loud clap while blindfolded.

image credits: Dad

Soon, Starship appeared. Dad had expected it to approach from the west, but from where we stood, it looked as though it was coming straight down. It descended quickly, lit its engines, and adjusted its position. Then it was caught! We had come to watch the ship’s recovery, and we had actually seen it happen! I felt amazed.

Finally, it was time to head home. We had forgotten exactly where we had parked, but when Dad pressed the remote, the car’s lights flashed only a few feet away.

On the drive back, I wrote much of this journal, watched the wind turbines spin, and ate snacks, including some Mom had brought back from China. Dad felt rested enough to keep driving without a nap, and we stopped for dinner along the way. This time, I got bored easily, but after some boredom I managed to fall asleep.

I fell asleep around 8:00 p.m. and woke up when we were nearly home. Still drowsy, I went upstairs to bed and slept until Sunday morning.

The trip was over, but I came home with great memories of Starship, the beach, and our time together.
